## Tumor

Let‘s review some cancer terms. A tumor is an abnormal growth. (11)

Tumors can be either benign or malignant

## Tumor

A benign tumor of the stomach may grow over time, but it won’t ever spread anywhere else (12)

## Tumor

A malignant tumor of the stomach has the potential to spread elsewhere in the body. This could be to the lymph nodes, the lungs, the liver, so the bone or spread inside the abdominal cavity..(13)

## Cancer

## Upper Endoscopy = EGD

In most cases, the diagnosis of esophageal cancer is made by endoscopy, also called and EGD for esophago-gastroduodenoscopy (15)

Under sedation, a flexible scope is passed through the mouth into the stomach, which allows viewing the inside of the stomach.

If a tumor is found in the stomach, a small portion can be removed for analysis, which is called a biopsy

## Biopsy

The biopsy is examined by a pathologist, who will determine whether the tumor is benign or whether it is malignant, meaning a cancer (16)

## Staging

If the biopsy shows cancer, the next step is staging. Staging is the process of finding out the size of the tumor and whether or not there has been spread to the lymph nodes or other places in the body.(17)

## Staging

The recommended cancer treatment depends upon the stage (18)

Once the stage has been determined, it will be possible to determine the best therapy

## Staging

Staging is going to focus on three areas: (19)

The first is the tumor. How large is the tumor? And more importantly, how deeply has it grown into the wall of the esophagus?

The second is whether there is spread to the lymph nodes.

The third is whether there is spread to other parts of the body

##

##

##

## Carcinomatosis

An additional pattern of potential spread is carcinomatosis (58). This is spread inside the abdominal cavity.

## Carcinomatosis

Cancer can grow in small nodules no larger than a grain of rice, and frequently can't be seen on CT or PET scans. (59)

## Carcinomatosis

Detection of carcinomatosis requires looking inside the abdodminal cavity with a small surgical procedure called laparoscopy (60)

## Laparoscopy

Laparoscopy is a minor surgical procedure performed under a general anesthetic as an outpatient

## Laparoscopy

## Treatment

We will briefly summarize treatments:

Superficial cancers are T1 and limited to the mucosa. These can usually be treated with endoscopic therapy alone. Surgery can be avoided in many of these cases. (66)

## Treatment

Localized cancers are T2 tumors that invade the muscle and are treated with surgery, also termed gastrectomy (67)

## Treatment

Locally-advanced cancers are T3 or Node-positive and are usually treated with chemotherapy first, followed by surgery (68)

## Treatment

Metastatic cancers are treated initially with intravenous chemotherapy. (69)

## Treatment

## Peritoneal Metastasis (M1)

After initial treatment with chemotherapy, some patients are candidates for additional therapy (71)

## Peritoneal Metastasis (M1)

Surgery is used in some patients with metastatic disease after initial chemotherapy. (72)

## Peritoneal Metastasis (M1)

Gastrectomy can be used to remove the tumor in the stomach (73)

## Peritoneal Metastasis (M1)

Cytoreductive surgery can be used to remove areas of spread of cancer within the abdomen (74)

## Peritoneal Metastasis (M1)

In some patients, chemotherapy can be administered into the abdominal cavity, also known as intraperitoneal chemotherapy (75)

## Peritoneal Metastasis (M1)

Intraperitoneal chemotherapy can be administered as a surgical procedure called HIPEC (76)

## Peritoneal Metastasis (M1)

Intraperitoneal chemotherapy can also be administered as an outpatient after surgical placement of a intraperitoneal catheter (77)
