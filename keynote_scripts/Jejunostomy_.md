## Jejunostomy

The topic for this video is a feeding jejunostomy tube

## Slide 3

We will start with a review of anatomy.  Food travels from the esophagus to the stomach, and finally into the jejunum, the first part of the small intestine.

## Slide 4

Cancers in the esophagus or stomach can in some cases ause difficulty with eating and in extreme cases can cause weight loss

Some patients with cancer of the esophagus or stomach can benefit from a feeding tube to help with nutrition

## Slide 5

There are two basic kinds of feeding tubes:

A gastrostomy tube is placed through the skin into the stomach

A jejunostomy tube is placed through the skin into the jejunum, or small intestine

## How Much Formula Do I Need?

## Slide 21

## Rate and Duration

#### Slide content

5 cartons:  240mL/carton x 5 = 1200mL
50mL/hour x 24 hours = 1200mL

Your dietitian will calculate how many cartons of tube feeds you will need

Let’s assume that you will need 5 cartons of tube feeds, where each carton is 240mL

This would total 1200mL of formula per day

One way to take in 5 cartons would be to run the tube feeds continuously, 24 hours per day.

This would require a rate of 50mL per hour, because 50 x 24 is 1200.

So what if you wanted to get the feeding in within 12 hours?  You would need to run the pump at 100mL/hour.

For many patients, this won’t work, because the small intestine can’t handle that much formula at a time


## 5 cartons Tube feeds

#### Slide content

24 hours/day: 50mL/hour→Inconvenient
16 hours/day: 75mL/hour
12 hours/day: 100mL/hour→Diarrhea/cramps

A slower rate of 50mL/hour is inconvenient, as it may require being hooked up to the pump all day.

On the other hand, a faster rate of 100mL/hr  would be more convenient, but carries the risk of diarrhea and cramps.

## Slide 29

Too high a rate of tube feeds will overwhelm the jejunum, and the result is cramps and diarrhea. 

## 5 cartons Tube feeds

#### Slide content

 
16 hours/day: 75mL/hour


Running tube feeds for 16 hours a day is for many patients a good compromise between convenience and good tolerance of tube feeds. Most patients can tolerate 75mL/hour of tube feeds.

## Starting Tube Feeds

#### Slide content

Feeds need to be started slowly
Allows jejunum to adjust to the feedings
Typically start at 30mL/hour (continuous)

When starting tube feeds for the first time it is important to start slowly to allow the small intestine, the jejunum, to get adjusted.

We typically start at 30mL/hour and run them continuously, meaning 24 hours per day. 

## Starting Tube Feeds

#### Slide content

Rate is increased 10mL/hour every 8-12 hours

The rate of administration of the formula is then increased 10mL/hour every 8 to 12 hours up to the goal rate.

#### Slide content

## Nocturnal Tube Feeds

#### Slide content

Tube feeds from 6pm to 10am
Disconnected from pump 10am to 6pm

A typical schedule would be to connect the feeding tube to the pump at 6pm and run tube feeds at night from 6pm to 10am.

This would allow being disconnected from the pump from 10am to 6pm.

## Additional Water

#### Slide content


240mL  x 4 times per day = 960mL
8oz        x 4 times per day = 32oz



Most patients need about a liter of additional water a day, which can be taken either by mouth of through the feeding tube

One way to do this is to take 240mL of water 4 times per day, which is about a liter total water

This is the same as 8 ounces 4 times per day, which totals about 32 ounces.

Your care team will help you determine how much water to administer and how often.

## Slide 40

## What if my tube falls out?

#### Slide content

Contact your care team immediately
Go to your local emergency room

If your jejunostomy tube falls out, it is important to take action right away.

Call your care team for instructions
If you can’t reach your care team, go immediately to a local emergency room. 

The skin will start to close over within a matter of hours, which can make it difficult to replace the tube.
