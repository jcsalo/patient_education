## Jejunostomy

The topic for this video is a feeding jejunostomy tube

## Slide 3

We will start with a review of anatomy.  Food travels from the esophagus to the stomach, and finally into the jejunum, the first part of the small intestine.

## Slide 4

Cancers in the esophagus or stomach can in some cases cause difficulty with eating and even weight loss

In these cases, a feeding tube can help with nutrition

## Slide 5

There are two basic kinds of feeding tubes:

A gastrostomy tube is placed through the abdominal wall into the stomach

A jejunostomy tube is placed through the abdominal wall into the jejunum, or small intestine

The stomach sends a small amount of food at a time into the jejunum, much like this conveyor belt.

## Slide 16

Just like a conveyor belt, it’s be easy for the jejunum to get overloaded if it receives too much food at once.

## Slide 17

In order to avoid overloading the jejunum, the formula is administered at a slow, steady rate with a pump over several hours

## How Much Formula Do I Need?

An important question is “How much formula do I need?”

## Slide 21

Your dietitian will calculate how much formula you need and will also make recommendations for the rate and duration of tube feeds, and for the administration of additional water.

## Rate and Duration

Your dietitian will calculate how many cartons of tube feeds you will need

Let’s assume that you will need 5 cartons of tube feeds, where each carton is 240mL

This would total 1200mL of formula per day

One way to take in 5 cartons would be to run the tube feeds continuously, 24 hours per day.

This would require a rate of 50mL per hour, because 50 x 24 is 1200.

## 5 cartons Tube feeds 1200mL

So we could run tube feeds 24 hours per day at 50mL/hour.  This would work fine…

## 5 cartons Tube feeds

The problem with this is it’s inconvenient. 

If you want to go for a walk or go shopping, you would need to take your pump with you,

## Slide 25

If you can avoid it, you don’t want to feel like you’re chained to your infusion pump.

## 5 cartons Tube feeds

An alternative would be to increase the rate and decrease the time.
You could increase the rate to 75mL/hour, which would only take 16 hours.  You would have 8 hours a day disconnected from the pump.

## 5 cartons Tube feeds

So what if you wanted to get the feeding in within 12 hours?  You would need to run the pump at 100mL/hour.

For many patients, this won’t work, because the small intestine can’t handle that much formula at a time


## 5 cartons Tube feeds

For most patients, 100mL/hour will overwhelm the jejunum, causing diarrhea and cramps

## Slide 29

Running the tube feeds too fast is like overloading a conveyor belt.

## 5 cartons Tube feeds

Running tube feeds for 16 hours a day is for many patients a good compromise between convenience and good tolerance of tube feeds. Most patients can tolerate 75mL/hour of tube feeds.

## Starting Tube Feeds

When starting tube feeds for the first time it is important to start slowly to allow the small intestine, the jejunum, to get adjusted.

We typically start at 30mL/hour and run them continuously, meaning 24 hours per day. 

## Starting Tube Feeds

The rate of administration of the formula is then increased 10mL/hour every 8 hours up to the goal rate.

## Nocturnal Tube Feeds

Once the feedings are running at the goal rate,
It is possible to convert to a nocturnal schedule, to run the feedings just at night

## Nocturnal Tube Feeds

Typically we aim to run the pump for 16 hours of feedings which leaves 8 hours to be disconnected from the pump

## Nocturnal Tube Feeds

A typical schedule would be to connect the feeding tube to the pump at 6pm and run tube feeds at night from 6pm to 10am.

This would allow being disconnected from the pump from 10am to 6pm.

## Water Flushes

Flushing the jejunostomy tube with water is important for two reasons:

To prevent dehydration…

## Water Flushes

… and to prevent clogging of the tube.

## Water Flushes

Water flushes are required…

At the start and end of tube feeds
and before and after the administration of medicines  

In addition, you will likely need to give additional water during the day

## Additional Water



Most patients need about a liter of additional water a day, which can be taken either by mouth of through the feeding tube

One way to do this is to take 240mL of water 4 times per day, which is about a liter total water

This is the same as 8 ounces 4 times per day, which totals about 32 ounces.

Your care team will help you determine how much water to administer and how often.

## Slide 40

Some pumps come with two bags, one for formula and one for water.

Your care team will tell you how much water to use, but it’s generally around one quart or 1000mL per day total


## Slide 41

## Slide 42

It’s important to avoid cold water, which can cause cramps

## Jejunostomy Tube

## What if my tube falls out?

If your jejunostomy tube falls out, it is important to take action right away.

Call your care team for instructions.

If you can’t reach your care team, go immediately to a local emergency room. 

The skin will start to close over within a matter of hours, which can make it difficult to replace the tube if too much time elapses.
