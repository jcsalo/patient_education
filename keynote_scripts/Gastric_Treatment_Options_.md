## Localized Gastric Cancer

*Localized* gastric cancers are T1b or T2

Endoscopic removal is not possible because of how deep they have invaded into the wall of the stomach

## Localized Gastric Cancer

These tumors are treated with surgery, known as gastrectomy.

## Locally-advanced Gastric Cancer

Locally-advanced gastric cancers are those for which there are concerns about the risk of spread to lymph nodes

## Locally-advanced Gastric Cancer

These cancers are either T3 or node-positive on staging

## Locally-advanced Gastric Cancer

In order to address the cancer in the lymph nodes, chemotherapy is usually administered both before and after surgery

We have a separate video which addresses locally-advanced gastric cancer

## Metastatic Gastric Cancer

As we discussed earlier, there are a number of different sites where gastric cancer can spread:
Liver
Lungs
Bone
Or inside the abdominal cavity called the peritoneum. This is also referred to as carcinomatosis.

## Metastatic Gastric Cancer

The primary treatment for metastatic gastric cancer is systemic therapy. These are drugs that can circulate throughout the bloodstream.

There are two types of systemic therapy:
Chemotherapy are drugs which directly kill cancer calls
Immunotherapy are drugs which stimulate your body’s immune system to kill cancer cells
Most systemic therapy is given intravenously

## Peritoneal Metastasis


Some patients may have metastatic disease limited to the abdominal cavity. In other words, they have metastatic disease limited to the peritoneum.

These patients are initially treated with systemic therapy. Some patients are candidates for additional therapy.;

## Peritoneal Metastasis

After treatment with systemic therapy, some patients are candidates for surgery

## Peritoneal Metastasis

Gastrectomy can be used to remove the tumor in the stomach

## Peritoneal Metastasis

Cytoreductive surgery can be used to remove areas of spread of cancer within the abdomen.  Cytoreductive surgery involves the removal of the cancer that has spread from the stomach to other areas in the abdomen.

## Intraperitoneal Chemoterapy 

In some patients with peritoneal metastasis, chemotherapy can be administered directly into the abdominal cavity, also known as intraperitoneal chemotherapy.


## Intraperitoneal Chemoterapy 


The rationale for this approach is that it chemotherapy administered into the abdominal cavity may have a better chance of reaching the cancer cells

## Intraperitoneal Chemotherapy

One method of administering chemotherapy directly into the abdomen is HIPEC, or heated intraperitoneal chemotherapy.

## Intraperitoneal Chemotherapy

This is a surgical procedure in which heated chemotherapy is used to bather the abdominal organs

## Intraperitoneal Chemotherapy

Another method is *outpatient* intraperitoneal chemotherapy.

An indwelling tube is placed into the abdomen, and then chemotherapy is administered through tube directly into the abdominal cavity.

## Peritoneal Metastasis (M1)

To summarize, patients with metastatic disease limited to the peritoneum are treated initially with chemotherapy.

In some cases, they may be candidates for additional chemotherapy administered directly into the abdomen or peritoneal cavity either during surgery or as an outpatient.
